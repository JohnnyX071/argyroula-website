Psychological and Psychometric Assessment

Evidence-Based Clinical & Psychodiagnostic Evaluation

Psychological Assessment is the foundational and indispensable first step of any therapeutic or counseling endeavor. Its objective is to acquire a comprehensive understanding of personality, psychological functioning, underlying difficulties, and the unique needs of an individual, couple, or family system. It encompasses clinical interviewing, developmental and personal history taking, behavioral observation, administration of standardized psychometric tools, and expert clinical appraisal.

Psychometric Assessment

Psychometric assessment is a specialized branch of psychological evaluation founded upon scientifically validated, standardized testing instruments. It supplies objective, empirical data regarding multiple facets of functioning, playing a vital role in differential diagnosis and the formulation of tailored, targeted therapeutic interventions.

Psychometric assessment contributes to:

Thorough investigation of therapeutic and clinical needs
Differential clinical appraisal and accurate diagnosis
Targeted, measurable treatment and intervention planning
Drafting expert witness reports and judicial evaluations
Enhanced self-awareness and meaningful personal growth

The Assessment Process:

1
Clinical Interview: Comprehensive history taking, case exploration, and clarification of the evaluation request.
2
Tool Administration: Completion of validated, standardized psychometric inventories tailored to the case.
3
Specialized Analysis: Computerized scoring, profiling, and expert clinical interpretation incorporating validity indicators.
4
Report Compilation: Comprehensive written report including quantitative results, clinical impressions, and actionable recommendations.
5
Feedback Session: Dedicated consultative conference discussing the findings, addressing questions, and guiding next steps.

The entire assessment procedure can be conducted seamlessly online.

Modern Psychometric Personality Assessment (MMPI-3)

The MMPI-3 (Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory-3) is the newest, most empirically validated, and internationally recognized psychometric instrument for evaluating adult personality structure and psychopathology.

Comprising 335 items, it renders a multidimensional, systematic mapping of psychological functioning, spanning emotional stability, internalizing/externalizing symptoms, behavioral patterns, and interpersonal tendencies. Furthermore, sophisticated validity scales rigorously examine response consistency and defensiveness.

Why MMPI-3 Over MMPI-2?

The MMPI-3 is not merely an abbreviated version of the MMPI-2; it is a substantially modernized and updated evolution engineered to meet contemporary scientific benchmarks and clinical exigencies.

New & Updated Scales: Integrates modern empirical research and broadens the scope of clinical and personality markers.
Refined Validity Metrics: State-of-the-art scales evaluating test-taking attitude, consistency, and protocol interpretability.
Contemporary Phrasing: Clearer, culturally adapted, and modernized item formulations.
Reduced Testing Time: 335 items (compared to 567 in MMPI-2), substantially reducing examinee fatigue while enhancing reliability.
Comprehensive Diagnostic Scope: Precise evaluation of contemporary presentations of distress and personality functioning.

The Critical Importance of Expert Interpretation

The utility of the MMPI-3 extends far beyond numeric item scores. Authentic clinical value resides in scientifically sound interpretation by an expert clinical psychologist who synthesizes findings with life context, anamnesis, and assessment goals. Scores do not constitute an automated diagnosis in isolation, but serve as an integral pillar of a rigorous, customized, and evidence-grounded psychological evaluation.

Interpersonal Relationships Assessment

Individuals | Couples | Families

Specialized Mapping of Interpersonal Dynamics & Relating Patterns

“Good relationships keep us happier and healthier.” — The Harvard Study of Adult Development (Waldinger & Schulz, 2023)

Extensive scientific literature highlights the profound influence of interpersonal relationships on physical and mental health, family harmony, occupational performance, and overall quality of life. However, relationships can present recurring challenges, emotional distances, conflicts, and maladaptive interactional cycles. Understanding the way we relate is the first fundamental step toward meaningful transformation.

Psychometric Instruments for Relationship Assessment

PROQ3

The Person’s Relating to Others Questionnaire: Assesses how an individual generally relates to others across eight distinct interpersonal dimensions, identifying maladaptive relational patterns.

Clinical Value: Fosters self-awareness, unveils unconscious recurring relational tendencies, and clarifies personal strengths and vulnerabilities.

CREOQ3

The Couple’s Relating to Each Other Questionnaire: Evaluates dyadic interaction patterns, assessing how partners relate to one another and their mutual perceptions.

Clinical Value: Unpacks conflict triggers, emotional disconnection, and interlocking relational cycles, fostering emotional intimacy and constructive communication.

FMIQ

Family Members’ Interrelating Questionnaire: Evaluates relational dynamics between parents and adult children within the family architecture.

Clinical Value: Highlights cross-generational interactional patterns, clarifies systemic distress points, and fosters healthy, supportive boundaries.

PPROQ3-SF

Person’s Positive Relating to Others Questionnaire – Short Form: Evaluates adaptive, constructive styles of relating to others.

Clinical Value: Maps positive interpersonal resources, strengthening relational resilience and supporting positive psychological growth.
