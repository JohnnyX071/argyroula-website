Professional Supervision in Interpersonal Relationship Assessment

From Training to Effective Clinical Application

Specialized scientific guidance in administering, interpreting, and applying interpersonal relationship assessment instruments in counseling and therapeutic practice! While formal training serves as the gateway to mastering Relating Theory, ongoing supervision is the crucial catalyst empowering clinicians to integrate these sophisticated psychometric models smoothly into day-to-day therapeutic interventions.

Within a safe, collaborative, and reflexive professional environment, supervisees deepen their interpretive acumen, refine clinical formulation, and cultivate heightened confidence in formulating empirically grounded therapeutic strategies.

Supervision is open exclusively to clinicians and practitioners who have successfully completed the specialized postgraduate training in Relating Theory and its associated psychometric instruments.

Supervision Objectives

The primary aim is to cultivate the practitioner’s competence in synthesizing complex relational metrics, grounding them within the unique clinical tapestry of each client, and translating data into actionable intervention plans:

Ensuring rigorous administration, scoring, and ethical usage of instruments
Deepening multi-layered score interpretation and pattern discernment
Bridging empirical test findings directly with clinical formulation
Tailoring customized therapeutic and counseling interventions
Elevating clinical decision-making, judgment, and practitioner competence
Maximizing overall therapeutic efficacy and client positive outcomes

Key Focus Areas of Supervision

Throughout the supervisory process, participating professionals are supported to:

  • Present challenging clinical cases and receive individualized academic and clinical feedback.
  • Master the nuances of questionnaire scoring, Octagon profiling, and graphic interpretation.
  • Unpack interpretive perplexities and complex cross-scale interactions.
  • Deconstruct intricate interpersonal dynamics across individuals, couples, and family units.
  • Synthesize psychometric data into robust psychotherapeutic treatment plans.
  • Manage high-conflict, rigid, or resistant relational configurations.
  • Establish clear, measurable relational milestones for intervention tracking.
  • Exchange clinical insights, peer experiences, and best practices.
  • Hone clinical reasoning, self-reflection, and evidence-grounded discernment.
  • Enhance therapeutic alliance durability through systemic relational awareness.

Delivery Formats

Individual Supervision: One-on-one sessions tailored directly to the clinician's caseload.
Group Supervision: Dynamic group case discussions fostering rich peer exchange.
Online Modality: High-security virtual video conferences accommodating clinicians worldwide.
In-Person: Scheduled consultations at the clinical premises.

Target Audience

Supervision is open to qualified healthcare and mental health practitioners who have finalized the accredited training in Relating Theory:

Clinical Psychologists

Psychotherapists

Mental Health Counselors

Social Workers

Clinicians Utilizing Relational Metrics in Practice

Clinical Supervisor

Dr. Argyroula Kalaitzaki, Full Professor of Clinical Psychology at the Hellenic Mediterranean University, possesses specialized international credentials in Relating Theory (John Birtchnell, 1993/1996) and its psychometric measures. Her scholarly work has made pioneering international contributions to the validation, standardization, and implementation of these instruments across clinical populations.

Supervision is not an evaluation of the clinician; it is a collaborative sanctuary of reflection, inquiry, and growth, enhancing clinical competence and the caliber of client care.
